## TL;DR

1. **The session signer decides almost everything about money transmission.** If Blunts never touches user funds and the user alone can move them, Blunts is a software provider:
   - no FinCEN MSB registration;
   - no state money transmitter licenses (MTLs);
   - no NY BitLicense;
   - no California DFAL license.

   A server key that can **move USDC or dShares on its own** changes that answer:
   - it is arguably **"total independent control"** under FinCEN's test [2];
   - it is **"control"** of virtual currency "on behalf of others" in NY [4][5];
   - it is **"control"** under California's DFAL, which defines control as the "power to execute unilaterally … a digital financial asset transaction" [18][19].

   California's application window closed **2026-07-01**. A **new** business now needs the DFAL license *before* serving Californians [19]. **Recommendation:** ship v1 with **no unilateral server signer**. Every money-moving transaction should be user-signed, with a passkey tap per fill or a user-signed standing order that the server can only relay. If you must keep a signer, scope it by policy so it can only sign **Dinari buy orders from the user's own USDC**. It must never be able to send assets anywhere else. Get a written opinion before launch.
2. **NY doesn't need a Blunts license if the design stays non-custodial.**
   - NYDFS says plainly that writing self-custody wallet software "would not, in and of itself, require a BitLicense." A wallet where "you kept custody of other people's funds" likely would [4].
   - zerohash (BitLicensed in NY) is the entity that buys and sells USDC for NY residents. Blunts doesn't receive fiat, so NY's money-transmitter law (Banking Law Art. 13-B) isn't triggered [6].
   - **Cash App's stablecoin feature is not available in NY** [8], so NY users need the zerohash bank path.
3. **Dinari and NY:** Dinari Securities is registered as a broker-dealer in NY and is headquartered at 260 Madison Ave, NYC [9]. Its US integration guide lists **no state carve-outs** [10]. **Nothing found says NY residents are excluded from dShares, and nothing found says NY has specific tokenized-stock restrictions.** Get written confirmation from Dinari **[UNVERIFIED]**. The NY-specific risk is the **Martin Act**: NY securities fraud can be proven **without intent to deceive**, and the AG is actively defending state power over tokenized securities [11][12].
4. **RIA route (fee A): Blunts can register with the SEC, but probably not through the internet-adviser exemption as the product is designed today.**
   - The amended Rule 203A-2(e) requires advice "generated by … software-based models … based on personal information each client supplies" [13][14]. "Everyone gets QQQ" is a weak fit.
   - **Two better routes:**
     - the **multi-state exemption** (Rule 203A-2(d), required to register in 15 or more states), bridged by the **newly-formed-adviser** exemption (203A-2(c), 120 days) [14];
     - adding a real questionnaire that chooses among two or three allocations.
   - Once registered with the SEC, NY needs only a **$200/yr notice filing after the 6th NY client** [15]. **NY has no de minimis exemption beyond 5 clients.**
   - **Custody warning:** an RIA whose server key can move client dShares probably has **custody** under Rule 206(4)-2, and a Privy wallet isn't a qualified custodian. This is another reason to drop the unilateral signer [20].
5. **Fee B (fixed platform fee from Dinari) is the standard FINRA 2040 pattern.** It's acceptable if it is truly **fixed**: not per trade, not per funded account, not a share of AUM or commissions.
   - Dinari must document a "reasonable basis," ideally a legal opinion [21].
   - A per-funded-account bounty is the riskiest "fixed" variant, because the SEC treats success-based pay as broker pay [22].
   - The SEC staff's April 2026 statement on crypto user interfaces sets a similar tone: an interface may be paid **only by the user**, not by anyone else based on transactions [23][24].
6. **Sharing profit with Apple:**
   - **Apple's standard App Store commission on an in-app subscription is fine.** It's a distribution/payment cost, not referral pay.
   - **A custom deal paying Apple more for users it sends, or paying it a share of anything tied to trades or balances, is not fine.**
     - If Blunts is an RIA, that makes Apple a paid **promoter** (SEC Marketing Rule disclosures; NY requires solicitors to register [15]).
     - If the money comes from Dinari, it makes Apple an unregistered recipient of **transaction-based compensation**.
   - A cut of *users'* investment gains can't go to Apple or anyone else (file 12).
7. **Federal status, Sept 2026:**
   - **GENIUS Act** takes effect the earlier of **2027-01-18** or 120 days after final rules [25]. The OCC's yield ban reaches issuers' affiliates and "related third parties," but **not** unaffiliated apps paying their own promos [26][27].
   - From **2028-07-18**, service providers may offer only permitted stablecoins to US persons. Self-custody transactions are carved out [28][29].
   - **CLARITY Act failed cloture 49–50 on 2026-09-15** [30]. It is not law.
   - **SEC Innovation Exemption (2026-09-17)** gives relief to trading *venues* and AMM liquidity providers. It **does not cover Blunts** [31][32].
   - **Uniform MTMA:** about 31–35 states have adopted it. **NY has not** [33][34].
8. **Launch cost (licenses held by partners, Blunts non-custodial):**
   - One-time: roughly **$60–180k**.
   - Recurring: roughly **$60–250k/yr**, plus Dinari's $2k/month.
   - Time: about **10–16 weeks** of legal/licensing critical path, running alongside Dinari onboarding (§6).
   - If Blunts ever needs its own MTLs plus BitLicense plus DFAL: **$1–3M+ and 18–24+ months**. Don't design into that.

## 1. New York

### 1.1 Does Blunts need a BitLicense?

NY's rule (23 NYCRR 200.2(q)) defines "Virtual Currency Business Activity" to cover five things [5]:
1. receiving VC for transmission, or transmitting it;
2. "storing, holding, or maintaining custody or **control** of Virtual Currency **on behalf of others**";
3. buying and selling VC as a customer business;
4. exchange services;
5. controlling, administering or issuing a VC.

The rule also says: "The development and dissemination of software in and of itself does not constitute Virtual Currency Business Activity" [5]. **"Control" isn't defined in Part 200.** NYDFS's FAQ draws the line at self-custody vs. custody [4].

| Blunts fact pattern | NY analysis | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Privy embedded wallet, user-controlled.** Keys are sharded, the user authenticates, and Blunts can't move funds | This is "software that allows customers to self-custody," which NYDFS says doesn't in itself require a BitLicense [4]. Privy says the app never sees the private key [35] | **No BitLicense** *(counsel to confirm)* |
| **(b) Server session signer that can sign alone** | Privy's docs pitch session signers for acting "while a user is offline" [35]. A Blunts key that can move a NY user's USDC without that user's live approval is plausibly "maintaining … **control** … on behalf of others." Scoping policies narrows the risk but doesn't clearly remove it **[UNVERIFIED; no NYDFS guidance on delegated signers found]** | **High risk.** For NY: no unilateral signer, or get a NY counsel opinion first |
| **(b′) Signer that can only co-sign a user-initiated, user-signed request** (multi-sig-style validation) | This matches FinCEN's multi-sig reasoning: the provider "does not have total independent control" [2]. NYDFS usually follows the substance of control. No NY ruling found **[UNVERIFIED]** | **Low–medium risk** |
| **(c) zerohash does the fiat↔USDC step for NY users** | zerohash holds the NY BitLicense and is the principal for the buy or sell [7]. Blunts doesn't buy or sell VC "as a customer business" and doesn't receive fiat | **No Blunts license**, provided Blunts never takes customer funds and zerohash's contract makes zerohash the principal |
| **USDC sent from the user's wallet to Dinari to buy dShares** | This is a user payment to a seller, sent by the user. It falls within the consumer/merchant exemption on the user's side (200.3(c)) [4]. Whether *Dinari* needs NYDFS authority to accept USDC is Dinari's question. Nothing found shows a Dinari BitLicense [9] **[UNVERIFIED; ask Dinari]** | Not Blunts' license |
| **dShares themselves** | These are securities. NYDFS supervises VC firms, while the NY AG polices securities (Martin Act). No NY guidance found treating tokenized equities as "virtual currency" **[UNVERIFIED]** | Dinari's registration covers the securities side |

**2026 status of NY crypto rules:**
- **No amendments to Part 200 in 2026 were found.** NYDFS works mainly through industry letters. Recent ones cover **custody structures** (2025-09-30) and **blockchain analytics** (2025-09-17) [36]. Both apply to licensees, not to Blunts.
- **Proposed 23 NYCRR Part 202 (2026-06-09)** creates "Authorized Payment Stablecoin Issuers." It is built to be certified as "substantially similar" to GENIUS [37][38]. It governs **issuers**, not apps.
- **USDC for NY residents** goes through a BitLicensed partner's approved coin-listing policy. USDC reportedly isn't on the public NYDFS "Greenlist" [39] **[UNVERIFIED as of 2026]**, so availability depends on each partner. **Confirm in writing that zerohash (and Coinbase, if used) sells USDC on Arbitrum to NY residents.**
- **Proposed NY "CRYPTO Act" (S8901)** would make unlicensed VC business activity a crime, up to a Class C felony at ≥ $1M/yr. It has sat in the Senate Banks Committee since 2026-01-14 [40][41]. It isn't law, but it shows NY's appetite for enforcement. Treat the NY signer question as a criminal-exposure question if the bill passes later.

### 1.2 NY money transmitter license (Banking Law Art. 13-B)

- §641 requires a license to "receiv[e] money for transmission." It exempts agents of a licensee and **agents of a payee** [6].
- **Blunts never receives dollars:**
  - The bank path is user → zerohash → USDC to the user's wallet.
  - The withdrawal path is the user's wallet → zerohash → the user's bank.
- **No NY MTL is needed** as long as no dollars touch a Blunts account. That includes fee collection: Dinari collects its fees, or the RIA fee is billed through Apple or card.
- **Trap to avoid:** "Blunts fronts the deposit" or "instant credit" features where Blunts advances USDC and is repaid from ACH. That is Blunts receiving money for transmission, and possibly lending. **Don't build it without counsel.**

  - Its main office is at 260 Madison Ave, New York [9].
  - Its US partner guide defines eligible customers as US citizens, residents and tax persons, with **no state exclusions** [10].

## 2. FinCEN (federal) MSB analysis

**The test.** A money transmitter is anyone who accepts "currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person" and transmits it "to another location or person by any means" [1]. Moving a user's own value to *another location* counts.

For wallets, FinCEN looks at four things [2 §4.2]:
- (a) who owns the value;
- (b) where it's stored;
- (c) whether the owner interacts with the payment system directly;
- (d) whether the intermediary has **"total independent control over the value."**

| Blunts activity | Analysis | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Provides Privy wallet software; the user signs | "Unhosted wallet": the owner has total independent control. The provider isn't a money transmitter [2 §4.2.1] | **Not an MSB** |
| Server co-signs only after the user has signed a request | This is the multi-sig provider model, which "does not have total independent control" and "is not a money transmitter" [2 §4.2.2] | **Not an MSB** if truly conditional on the user's signature |
| Server signer can move value alone (even under a scope policy) | The intermediary has independent control over the value for that scope. FinCEN warns the answer is the same "regardless of the label" [2 §4.2.2]. The risk is moderate if the only allowed target is a Dinari buy for the same user. It is higher if the signer can send USDC out, e.g., auto-sweeping to Cash App **[UNVERIFIED; no ruling on scoped delegated keys]** | **Risk of MSB status.** Needs FinCEN registration, an AML program, SAR/CTR filing and state MTLs, or a redesign |
| "Integral to the sale of goods or services" exemption (ff)(5)(ii)(F) | Only covers the **seller's own** acceptance of funds (the "person who is accepting and transmitting" must be the one selling) [1][2]. Dinari sells the dShares, not Blunts. FinCEN reads exemptions "strictly" [2] | **Doesn't help Blunts** |
| Payment processor / agent of payee (ff)(5)(ii)(B) | Requires an agreement with the seller **and** settlement through a clearance and settlement system between BSA-regulated institutions. FinCEN says CVC payment processors generally **don't** qualify [2 §4.6] | **Doesn't help** for USDC flows |
| SEC-registered persons (ff)(8)(ii) | Persons "registered with, and functionally regulated or examined by, the SEC" are **not MSBs** [1]. An **SEC-registered RIA** is arguably covered. A **state**-registered RIA isn't **[counsel; a narrow federal-only benefit]** | Helps only against federal MSB status. **State MTL laws have no general RIA exemption** |
| FinCEN investment-adviser AML rule | Postponed to **2028-01-01** (file 03) | Not a 2026 obligation |

**Bottom line:** keep Blunts out of the flow of funds and make every value-moving signature user-controlled. Then Blunts isn't an MSB. The partners are the MSBs or regulated firms: zerohash is a registered MSB with MTLs and a BitLicense; Block/Cash App is too; Dinari is a broker-dealer.

## 3. State money transmission, the MTMA and California DFAL

### 3.1 Summary across 50 states + DC

| Flow | Who's licensed | Does Blunts need an MTL? |
|---|---|---|
| Cash App USD → USDC → user's Privy wallet | Block (Cash App's licenses) | No. Block and the user do the transfer |
| Bank → zerohash → USDC → user's wallet | zerohash (51 jurisdictions plus BitLicense) | No, if the zerohash contract makes zerohash the principal and Blunts never receives funds |
| User wallet → Dinari (buy) / Dinari → user wallet (sell proceeds) | Dinari (broker-dealer). Most state MTL laws exempt registered broker-dealers **[varies by state]** | No, if user-signed |
| User wallet → Cash App or zerohash (withdrawal) | User-signed transfer; the ramp is licensed | No, if user-signed. **Yes-risk if a server key does it** |
| Fee collection | Apple / card processor, or Dinari | No, if no pooled Blunts account receives customer funds |

**Virtual-currency-specific state laws to watch:**
- About 20 states now treat VC transmission as money transmission by statute or guidance **[UNVERIFIED count]**.
- Several MTMA enactments include a **virtual-currency title** tied to "control" (e.g., Minnesota, North Dakota, Maine; Delaware's pending bill) [34].
- **In every one of them, the licensing hook for a wallet provider is "control."** Getting the signer design right fixes all of them at once.

### 3.2 Uniform Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA)

- **Adoption:** CSBS counts 31 states with full or partial enactments. A later 2026 tracker counts **35 by Aug 2026** [33][34].
- **Recent adoptions:**
  - 2025: Colorado, Massachusetts (effective 2026-01-01), Mississippi, Nebraska, Virginia (effective 2026-07-01).
  - 2026: Louisiana (effective 2026-07-01), Oklahoma (effective 2026-11-01). Alaska and Delaware are pending [34].
- **New York has not adopted it** and keeps Art. 13-B plus the BitLicense.
- **What it means for Blunts:**
  - More uniform definitions, the agent-of-payee exemption, and a single multistate exam.
  - **It doesn't create a non-custodial-software exemption beyond "no control," and it doesn't change the analysis above.**

### 3.3 California DFAL (the sleeper issue)

- **Scope:** licensing is required to exchange, transfer or **store** digital financial assets with or for California residents [18].
- **Definition of control:** "power to execute unilaterally or prevent indefinitely a digital financial asset transaction," confirmed by the 2026 SB 97 clean-up [19].
- **Timing:** firms that filed complete applications by **2026-07-01** can keep operating while the application is pending. **Businesses formed after that date must be licensed before operating** [19].
- **Exemptions:**
  - "Connectivity software or computing power" providers and pure "data storage or security services" providers, **only if they don't otherwise engage in the activity** [44].
  - Broker-dealers are exempt [18]. That covers Dinari, but not Blunts.
- **Consequence:** a Blunts server key able to sign alone could mean **"store/transfer" for Californians, and no license means no California launch**. With user-only signing, the software exemption and the no-control reading apply **[counsel]**.

## 5. FINRA 2040 and the fixed platform fee (fee B)

- **Rule 2040(a):** a FINRA member may not pay "any compensation, fees, concessions, discounts, commissions or other allowances" to a person who would have to register because of the payment. **Supplementary .01:** the member must "reasonably support" that conclusion through SEC guidance, a no-action letter, or a **legal opinion** from independent counsel [21].
- **What makes it transaction-based (bad):**
  - a percentage of commissions or sell fees;
  - per-trade fees;
  - per-funded-account bounties;
  - a percentage of AUM or deposits;
  - anything that rises when users trade more.

  SEC letters treat success-based pay as the "hallmark" of broker activity, "even if the compensation is paid only once" [22]. **No 2026 finder exemption exists**; the 2020 proposal was never adopted [46].
- **What Dinari can defensibly pay (good), strongest first:**
  1. A **flat monthly platform or licensing fee** that doesn't vary with accounts, trades or AUM, set for a term (e.g., 12 months). The best form is a **net reduction of the $2k/month API fee** rather than a payment to Blunts.
  2. **Fair-market-value fees for real services** Blunts performs for Dinari (KYC data capture, first-line support, OFAC/wallet screening), with documented cost-based pricing.
  3. **Tiered fixed fees** re-set at most annually. These are riskier because tiers track account counts **[counsel]**.
- **Also required:**
  - Dinari discloses the arrangement (Reg BI / Form CRS conflicts).
  - If Blunts is also an RIA, disclose it in ADV Part 2A. Payments from the broker-dealer to the adviser that recommends it are a conflict.
- **NY overlay:** §359-e "salesman" registration is triggered by *selling/offering* activity. Fixed pay plus no solicitation of specific trades is the defensive position [43].
- **Decision:** fee B works for launch **if** the fee is fixed and not per account. But Blunts then earns **nothing variable**. Fee A (RIA) is the only way Blunts earns more as balances grow (file 12 §1.6).

## 6. The founder wants to share a cut of profit with Apple

| Scenario | Legal read |
|---|---|
| **Apple's standard IAP commission** on a Blunts subscription (15% small-business or 30%) | **Fine.** It's a distribution and payment cost applied to all IAP, not pay for referring advisory clients. The flat subscription is also a valid RIA fee under §205 (not performance-based). **Side benefit:** billing through Apple means Blunts never debits the wallet, so no fee-deduction custody issue (§1.4). Whether Apple *requires* IAP for a financial-advice subscription, or lets you bill off-app, is **[UNVERIFIED]**. Brokerages usually bill from the account (file 04 §B) |
| **A bespoke revenue share** giving Apple a percentage of Blunts revenue from the app, **in exchange for featuring or promotion** | If Blunts is an **RIA**, Apple becomes a paid **promoter** under the SEC Marketing Rule: written disclosures, oversight, and **state solicitor/IAR registration** where required, **NY included** [15]. If the revenue is **Dinari-derived and transaction-linked**, passing a share on is indirect transaction-based pay to an unregistered person, the same 2040 / §15(a) problem. A fixed-fee Blunts passing along a share of *its own fixed income* is lower risk, but still promoter risk if Apple is "soliciting" **[counsel]** |
| **A share of users' investment gains** paid to Apple | **Prohibited:** a performance fee (§205) or transaction-based pay, same as file 12 Part 1. Apple wouldn't accept it anyway |
| **Equity or a profit-share partnership with Apple unrelated to user acquisition** | A corporate deal. No securities-intermediary issue in itself, but disclose it as a conflict if Apple distributes the app **[counsel]** |

**Recommendation:** the only safe "Apple gets a cut" is **Apple's standard store commission on a flat subscription**. Don't sign anything that pays Apple more for users it sends or for balances or trades.
