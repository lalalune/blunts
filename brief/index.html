BLUNTS

A savings app where $100 is a blunt and $1,000 is a band. The idea holds up. Three parts of the plumbing have to change before Americans can use it.

Prototype: click-through app Research, not legal advice. Get securities counsel before building.

The call

The loop works: deposit, watch a blunt fill, stack rolls, cash out whenever. Blunts can launch in the US within 12 to 18 months if it runs on regulated partners. The version in the pitch, with an offshore token and a 10% cut of profits, can't legally be sold to Americans.

Keep
The core loop and the math

$25 quarter, $100 blunt, $1K band. Blunts count money put in, and gains are shown separately. Only two buttons. No trading. That last part matters: regulators go after trading games, and this app has none.

Change
How it's built for the US

Replace the pooled BLUNT token with a model portfolio. Each user owns real fractional shares at a registered broker. On-chain comes later.

Drop for US
Profit cut, withholding, offshore

A share of profits is banned for everyday clients. Nobody can withhold capital-gains tax. Running a Cayman/BVI entity that serves Americans is how Binance and BitMEX ended up in enforcement actions.

The MVP

One asset, one pay-in, one payout. Every blunt buys QQQ. Money comes in and goes out through Cash App. A bank account is the backup, and only people who can't use Cash App ever see it.

Who sees what

  • Cash App user, not in New York: Cash App in, Cash App out. No bank needed.
  • New York, or no Cash App: adds a bank or uses Apple Pay (through Coinbase). Bank fills take 1–3 days, Apple Pay fills are instant, and sparks to a bank are instant.

Must-haves before launch

  • Send payouts only as native USDC on Arbitrum. Run a $1 test payout when someone saves their Cash App address. A payout on the wrong network can't be recovered.
  • Watch Arbitrum, Ethereum and Polygon for incoming deposits, with a "send it back" button for mistakes.
  • Queue sparks above Cash App's $10k-a-week receive limit.
  • Fit Dinari's required screens into the design: exact disclosure wording, 6 checkboxes, a live price before each order.
  • Dinari: $2k a month minimum, SOC 2 or equivalent, $0.20 per order (ask to be billed at actual gas instead).

Fee decision still open. A 10–20% cut of profits isn't legal for US retail users under this setup. It's either a banned adviser performance fee or broker pay Blunts can't receive. The two legal options: (1) Blunts registers as an adviser and charges 0.25–0.5% a year on the balance, collected only at spark, or (2) Dinari charges a flat fee on sparks and pays Blunts a fixed platform fee. The prototype still shows 10% of profit.

Shipping it

Yes, this can ship as a real app on iOS, Android and the web, in all 50 states plus DC, including New York. It takes one rule: Blunts never moves anyone's money on its own. Every fill and spark is signed by the user with Face ID, and our server only passes it along. That keeps Blunts out of money-transmitter, BitLicense and California crypto licensing.

Apple's cut
0% of deposits and trades

Apple and Google don't take a cut of brokerage money, and there's no legal way to give them a share of profit. Cut them in with an optional Blunts+ subscription for digital extras (themes, stash skins, stats), sold through in-app purchase. They keep 15%.

Apple Pay
Yes, through Coinbase

Blunts can't be the Apple Pay merchant for money going into investments. Coinbase's Headless Onramp can: Apple Pay (debit) buys USDC into the user's wallet with no Coinbase account. Free to us if Coinbase approves Blunts for its zero-fee USDC program.

App Review
The real risk

Apple rule 3.2.1(viii) wants investing apps submitted by the licensed firm. Send a letter from Dinari, its CRD number, a demo account and clean store art (money, no leaves). If Apple still says no, Blunts registers as an adviser. Google has no such rule.

Login

  • No username, no password. Face ID creates a passkey, and that is the account. A phone number is the required backup, for new phones and recovery.
  • "Sign in with Cash App" doesn't exist. Block offers no login for other apps. What we can do: save the user's Cash App address after a $1 test payout, so sparks just land there.
  • We can't skip identity. The broker has to verify every funded user (about 2 minutes, inside the app). The tutorial runs as a guest before that.

Web

  • The web is the full app, built from the same codebase (Expo) with the same passkey and the same wallet.
  • No App Review, so it's where the closed beta starts. On desktop, Cash App deposits become "scan this QR."
  • Weak spots: push only after "Add to Home Screen" on iPhone, and more phishing risk.

Launch order: web beta (non-NY Cash App) → add zerohash bank and NY → Android → iOS. About 16–18 weeks of build with 3 engineers, plus 3–6 weeks for partner approvals and review. Fixed costs are about $6.5–11k a month, driven by Dinari's $2k minimum. Full plan: research/19-ship-plan-feasibility.md.

Making money

Update: 1% on every fill and spark. The market (Americans 18–34) is about a $750M-a-year fee pool at 1% per move. The core audience (18–34 Cash App users who don't invest yet, about 22M people) is about $210M. At $600 a year put in, a user earns about $12 and nets about $2, which doesn't pay for a team. At $2,400 a year (fill every payday), a user nets about $33, and 300k users is about $12.5M revenue and $5M profit. The business depends on how much money each user moves, not on the fee rate. See research/22-tam-sam-revenue.md.

At $300–800 balances a user is worth about $4–5 a year at launch and about $12 at scale. Serving one costs about $4.50 a year. The fee alone won't pay for ads, so the plan layers revenue on top of it.

Design decisions in the prototype

  • Blunts track money put in, never market value. A market dip never un-rolls a blunt. Gains show up as a separate "Grown" number so users always know which part is theirs and which part the market gave them.
  • Celebrate saving, not selling. Deposits get flakes and stamps. Withdrawals get a quiet ash drift and an itemized receipt. This is also what the Massachusetts v. Robinhood settlement over confetti effects implies.
  • A tax stash instead of withholding. On gains you cash out, a slice goes into a separate balance that's still the user's money. It's settled at filing time. 0% is an option, since many users owe 0% on long-term gains.
  • No crypto vocabulary. No wallets, chains or tokens in the UI. "What's inside" shows the stock mix as plain percentages.
  • No credit cards. Card issuers often treat investing or crypto purchases as cash advances. Investing borrowed money is also how this audience gets hurt.
  • Fee model is a toggle in the prototype: your 10%-of-gains pitch next to a flat-fee version that's legal in the US.

If Blunts goes on-chain, users can deposit from the balance they already have for about a penny, with no chargebacks.

Unit economics: a user depositing $25 a week costs about $4–11 a year in rails. With a 10–20% cut of gains in a 10% year, that breaks even at an average balance of $200–1,000. In a down year there's no profit to share, so a flat or asset-based fee is also the more durable business.

For the US launch (next sections), the simpler path is bank and debit funding straight into the broker partner: Plaid plus the broker's ACH. Stablecoin rails matter for the international product and for phase 2.

The four blockers

Critical

The BLUNT token is a fund

The planA treasury pools deposits, buys a basket, and issues a redeemable token.
Why it breaksThat's a security and an unregistered investment company under the '40 Act. The SEC's Jan 2026 statement says tokenization changes nothing. Registering a fund costs $2–5M+ and takes 18–36 months.
InsteadUse the robo-adviser model, the way Acorns and Betterment work. Blunts registers as an investment adviser, and each user owns fractional shares at a broker partner. Rule 3a-4 keeps it out of fund territory. BLUNT becomes a portfolio name, not a token.
Critical

10% of profits

The planNo deposit or withdraw fees. Take 5–10% of gains.
Why it breaksAdvisers Act §205 bans performance fees except for "qualified clients": $1.4M invested with you or $2.7M net worth, as of June 29, 2026. Taking the cut at withdrawal doesn't change that.
InsteadA flat $3–5 a month, free below a threshold (for example, your first band), or a 0.25–0.35% asset fee. The line becomes "We never charge to put money in or take it out." Keep the profit cut only for a separate non-US product, where it may be allowed.
Critical

Cayman / BVI for Americans

The planSet up offshore, launch, sort out the US later.
Why it breaksUS law follows the investor. An English-language app with US culture, marketed to Americans on US app stores, is "directed selling" by definition. BitMEX paid $100M and its founders were charged, Binance paid over $4B, and KuCoin was barred from the US in March 2026. An offshore fund token is also a PFIC for US holders, a tax mess that contradicts "we handle your taxes."
InsteadUS-first through registered partners. If you want to ship faster, run a separate brand and entity for non-US markets (LatAm, Africa, Caribbean) with hard geoblocking and no US marketing, and treat it as a product lab.
High

"Withhold the taxes"

The planTake capital-gains tax out on the front end so users never deal with it.
Why it breaksUS brokers can't withhold capital-gains tax. The only withholding is 24% backup withholding when a user has no valid SSN or ITIN on file. Single filers under $49,450 of taxable income pay 0% on long-term gains in 2026, so blanket withholding would take money many users don't owe.
InsteadMake a Roth IRA blunt the flagship: growth is tax-free, and contributions can be withdrawn anytime. For taxable accounts: the tax stash, tax-smart lot selection, "hold 43 more days" nudges, broker-issued 1099s, and in-app filing through Column Tax or April.

Brand risk

The name can stay. The imagery needs care. Recreational cannabis is still Schedule I. Apple rejects apps that encourage drug use, Stripe prohibits cannabis, and broker partners have to approve your marketing under FINRA 2210 and the SEC Marketing Rule.

  • Position "blunt" as money slang, like "band." Keep smoke, joints, lighters and "get lit" copy out of the store listing, icon, screenshots and ads.
  • Keep the flake and ember effects inside the app, abstract and behind an age gate. Market to 21+, even if accounts open at 18.
  • Have the broker, bank and app-review contacts approve the brand guide in writing before you commit to it.
  • Keep a brand-safe name for ads and partner decks. Run a USPTO trademark search on BLUNTS in Class 36 now.
  • Expect the "weed stock app targets urban youth" headline. Answer it in advance with plain risk disclosures ("you can lose money"), no return promises, and a community advisory group.
